A LETTER · About 6 minutes

J · L By Jack & Lila Carver

Peptides, deep sleep, and the body you no longer recognize after 40.

Dear Reader,

If you found this site through a search at 6:42 a.m. with one cup of coffee already gone and another about to be — we know what you typed.

Some version of: why am I always tired. why can't I lose this weight. why does my body ache like this now. why do I not feel like myself anymore.

You are between thirty-five and sixty, and something has been shifting inside your body for the last few years that nobody warned you about and your doctor cannot name. You have probably been told it is stress. You have probably been told to lose some weight. You have probably been told you are not as young as you used to be.

None of those answers fit what you are actually feeling.

This site exists because we lived through that. Both of us. For three years. We are a husband and wife who spent our late forties feeling like strangers in our own bodies, exhausting our patience with five different doctors, and slowly figuring out what was actually happening — and then learning enough to do something about it.

We write what we wish someone had written for us, back when we were waking up tired and didn't have the words for why.

If this is your first visit, start with Letter #1.

It is the cornerstone letter — the one that explains the framework underneath everything else on this site. We call it The Repair Window — the 25-year stretch between 35 and 60 during which your body's overnight repair machinery quietly stops working the way it used to. Letter #1 explains what that means in plain English, why your doctor probably missed it, and three small things you can try tonight that we have seen meaningfully shift the tired-body wake-up within a week.

It is about thirteen minutes to read. It is the longest letter on the site. It is also the one we would hand to a friend at dinner.

Who this is for.

It is for the man who is waking up at 6:30 every morning feeling like he slept on a concrete floor, who has stopped recognizing the body in the mirror, and who keeps being told by his physician that everything looks fine on the labs.

It is for the woman who is forty-six and gaining weight she has never gained before in places she has never gained it, whose doctor said it was stress, who knows it is not stress.

It is for the couple who used to have a sex life and don't anymore, and who haven't said anything to each other about it because what is there to say.

It is for the dad who used to play basketball with his teenage son and now sits out the second game because his knees won't take it.

It is for anyone over thirty-five who has been quietly losing the version of themselves they used to know — and who suspects, correctly, that the answer involves something more specific than "more sleep" and "lower stress."

Who this is not for: people in their twenties looking for a lifestyle hack. People who want a 30-day program with a gradient header and a money-back guarantee. People who already know what's wrong with them and just want a supplement to buy. We can't help with any of that. There are sites for it. This isn't one.

Why we write.

We were not always this. Both of us spent our forties in the same drift — the slow, unannounced collapse of our recovery, our sleep, and the body composition we used to take for granted. Lila stopped sleeping through the night somewhere around forty-three. Jack stopped recovering from his Saturday workouts sometime in his mid-forties and started getting sore for five days at a time. Neither of us said anything to the other for months. We were both quietly assuming it was just what aging felt like.

It wasn't. It was a specific transition with a specific name and a specific set of mechanisms — and almost nobody was writing about it for adults like us in plain English. The information existed; you could find it scattered across endocrinology journals, biohacker forums, fragmented Instagram posts, and a few good books. We pieced it together over two years. Then we started writing it down, because we knew we weren't the only ones who needed it.

If you'd like the long version of how we got here, we wrote it down.

P.S.

We don't have a newsletter. We don't have a course. We don't have a supplement. We're not selling you anything in this letter or in the next one. The whole site is free to read and we have no plans to put anything behind a paywall.

Eventually, the writing turns into something — that is just what writing does. Probably we will end up with a small recommended-products page, and probably a longer-form guide. When that happens you'll know, because we'll have written a letter about it. Until then, we are doing the slow thing — the writing.

If something we wrote on this page made you feel less alone, the best thing you can do for us is forward this site to one person you think needs it. A spouse. A brother. A friend. Anyone over thirty-five who hasn't slept right in a year. The whole point of these letters is that nobody should have to live through this transition alone the way we did.
